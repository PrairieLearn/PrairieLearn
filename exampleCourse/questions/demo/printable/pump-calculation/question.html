8 points. A pump lifts $500\ \mathrm{kg}$ of water through a vertical height of $12\ \mathrm{m}$. Use $g=9.8\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$. The pump converts 70% of its electrical input energy into gravitational potential energy; ignore all other changes in the water's energy.

(a) 3 points. Calculate the increase in gravitational potential energy, in joules, using $\Delta U=mgh$. Show your substitution.

(b) 5 points. Calculate the electrical input energy, in kilowatt-hours. Use $1\ \mathrm{kWh}=3.6\times10^6\ \mathrm{J}$. Show how you account for efficiency and check whether the input exceeds the useful output.

(a) $\Delta U=(500)(9.8)(12)=58{,}800\ \mathrm{J}$.

(b) $$E_{\mathrm{in}}=\frac{58{,}800}{0.70}=84{,}000\ \mathrm{J}=\frac{84{,}000}{3.6\times10^6}=0.0233\ \mathrm{kWh}.$$

The electrical input is larger than the useful output because efficiency is below 100%.

Grading (8 points): substitution (1) and potential energy with units (2); divide by efficiency (2), convert joules to kilowatt-hours (2), and explain the energy comparison (1). Award method credit for consistent use of an earlier arithmetic error.
